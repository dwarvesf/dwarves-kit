## What worked
- **Fresh-context review is the load-bearing safety, not the tests.** The `/review` phase caught a CRITICAL `rm -rf node_modules/../..` traversal escape in SPEC-014 that per-task self-verification reported green on. It also proved (by injection-and-revert) that the read-only-tool guards actually bite in SPEC-021/022. The kit's "a fresh window beats the context that wrote the code" thesis validated on the kit's own work, repeatedly.
- **Spec -> validate -> review caught real issues at the cheapest point.** `/spec-validate` folded path-normalization, fail-open/closed, value-prop honesty (SPEC-014), the task-verifier boundary and base-ref (SPEC-021), and the doc-list source (SPEC-022) before any code. Cheaper than catching them in review or prod.
- **The numbering guard closed a class of bug.** After 3 silent SPEC/ADR collisions, a `test-meta.sh` assertion + a claim-at-commit convention turned the collision from a merge surprise into a loud CI failure. Guardrail-over-guidance, applied to the kit's own process.

## What hurt
- **Shared-branch concurrency taxed every step.** Two sessions on one working tree produced 3 number collisions, interleaved edits in shared files (WORKFLOW.md, architecture.md, CHANGELOG.md, execute.md), and forced a foreign-content check + path-scoped `git add` before every commit. Real overhead all cycle, and a latent risk of committing someone else's half-done work.
- **Self-verification gave false confidence.** Per-task test runs were green while a destructive bug sat in the SPEC-014 diff. "Tests pass" felt like done; it was not. Only the independent review surfaced it.
- **Verifiers stacked faster than evidence.** Three read-only verifiers (task / integration / doc) are now in the kit, all adopted on anticipated-not-observed pain. None has retro evidence of catching a real defect in actual downstream usage yet. This runs ahead of the kit's own "No speculative features" bar.

## Kit feedback
- **Numbering allocation was the root process bug.** "Assigned when the file is created" (docs/specs/README.md, pre-fix) guaranteed collisions under concurrency. Fixed this cycle to claim-at-commit + later-committer-renumbers + a dup guard. Watch whether claim-at-commit holds in practice or whether worktrees are needed.
- **The kit caught its own drift.** The doc-verifier shipped in SPEC-022 is exactly what would have flagged the stale `59/176` test-count comments in CLAUDE.md (actual 92/196), fixed in `71d8fc5`. Good sign the gap was real.
- **Anticipated-pain pattern across SPEC-013/021/022.** All three lean on "the gap is structural, the pain is not yet observed." That is defensible once, thrice it is a trend the retro should police. The PHILOSOPHY "no speculative features" bar and the verification-agent additions are in tension; the next retro is the forcing function.
- **No false-positive hook noise this cycle.** The new guards (secrets-guard, commit-format, phantom-impl, the safety-gate additions) shipped with regression tests proving they bite AND do not over-fire (independent tasks, .env.example, bare TODO, build-artifact rm). Calibration held.

## Feed forward
- The three action items are candidates for the next spec cycle (a "codify review gate" spec) and a PHILOSOPHY/WORKFLOW note (worktree-per-stream; the verifier-evidence bar before a 4th).
- ADR-worthy if pursued: making fresh-context review a hard full-lane gate (it would change the WORKFLOW contract).
